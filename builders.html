ContractorsPilot program, now enrolling

Bid from what your jobs actually cost.

In the pilot, it learns from your past projects, reads the new drawings and specs, and drafts the bid sheet: equipment, skilled labour, materials and unit prices. You review every line before it goes anywhere.

What the pilot sets out to do

  • Flag unit prices that lost money last time, before you repeat them.
  • Point out unclear details, buildability issues and grey-zone scope to raise with the engineer.
  • Suggest where you could sharpen the price when you want the job, for your review, and what new equipment you’d have to buy.

Submittals, sorted before they’re due.

In the pilot, it lists the submittals your drawings and specs require, gathers product data once you approve, drafts the emails to suppliers and assembles the packages. It also watches email and the schedule for red flags.

What the pilot sets out to do

  • Build the submittal list from your drawings and specs, not from memory.
  • Draft the supplier emails and packages. Nothing is sent without your OK.
  • Give early warning on schedule slips and money risks hidden in the inbox.

ContractorsPilot program, now enrolling

Your site, checked twice a day.

In the pilot, a docked drone flies your site on set morning and evening routes, flown under the supervision of a Part 107 remote pilot, subject to FAA authorization. We compare the photos with your drawings, specs and schedule, and flag what looks off to your engineer, contractor or PM.

What the pilot sets out to do

  • Catch work that doesn’t match the drawings while it’s still cheap to fix.
  • Show progress against the schedule without walking every floor.
  • Send each flag to the right person, with photos and the drawing reference.
Join the drone pilot →

What you get

  • Morning and evening photo sets on agreed routes
  • A flag list referenced to your drawings

What we need from you

  • One active site and its drawings and schedule
  • Site access, safety rules and airspace details

What it isn’t yet

  • Flights depend on FAA authorization for your site
  • Flags support your engineer; they don’t replace inspections
